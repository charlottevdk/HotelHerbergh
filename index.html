Social Direction — Proposal
Prepared for Hotel Herbergh

A social presence built around what Hotel Herbergh is actually trying to achieve.

Before proposing a content calendar, we want to agree on the goal. "Doing something with social media" can mean a growth engine, an ad infrastructure, or a brand statement — and each of those points to a different account, a different workload, and a different definition of success.

This page walks through two reference directions, a short set of questions to place you on the spectrum between them, and what to expect from each — including the new restaurant's role in the story.

Two starting points

Where should @hotelherbergh sit?

Neither direction is more "correct" — they optimize for different outcomes. Picking one early keeps the content plan honest.

Community & Growth

Frequent, low-friction posting. Staff on camera, trends joined quickly, day-to-day moments over polish. The account grows because it feels alive and local.

Strengths
  • Best path to real follower growth
  • Authentic, constant content supply from daily operations
  • Builds a recognisable, likeable team-first brand
Costs you carry
  • Needs a steady content pipeline — someone filming often
  • Staff must be willing (and available) to appear
  • Voice needs to stay consistent across many small posts
Please reference @aman on Instagram

Editorial & Exclusive

Sparse, deliberate, high-production posts. The account reads as a brand statement, not a feed to check daily. Prestige over frequency.

Strengths
  • Reinforces a premium, boutique positioning
  • Low ongoing content demand — a handful of shoots per quarter
  • Works well purely as ad infrastructure, even with few posts
Costs you carry
  • Slower, smaller follower growth — that's not the goal here
  • Less algorithmic discovery, since it isn't built for that
  • Every post needs to earn its place — no filler

Managing expectations

What each direction realistically gets you.

This is the part most proposals skip. Id'd rather set it now than explain it in month three.

If you choose Community & Growth

Expect follower growth to become visible within a few months, but only if posting stays consistent — this direction fails quietly if the content pipeline dries up. It also demands ongoing staff time, so it's a resourcing decision as much as a marketing one.

If you choose Editorial & Exclusive

Follower growth will be slow and that's not a failure — it's the trade-off for a low-content, high-control account. The channel still works perfectly well for targeted ads: ad reach runs on the pixel and audience data, not on organic follower count.

In short: a small, quiet, well-produced account can still be a fully functional ad engine. Follower count and ad performance are two different goals that happen to live on the same platform — choosing editorial doesn't mean choosing "less marketing," it means choosing a different metric to succeed by.
